# Executable Measurement Geometry

## Research claim

We study measurement-aware iterative structured refinement under deterministic
execution. The model repairs a structured geometric state whose execution defines
the target measurement, instead of directly correcting the resulting scalar.
An executable measurement graph is the representation, not the primary novelty.

```text
image -> landmark detector -> typed measurement program
      -> deterministic executor -> proposed geometric edit
      -> learned verifier -> accept / reject / stop -> quantitative result
```

## Implemented prototype

- Typed HVA and IMA measurement programs with named axes and explicit point roles.
- Deterministic executor in the original pixel coordinate frame.
- Gaussian and axis-rotation corruption operators.
- Program-aware oracle that selects a single point edit or a safe no-op.
- Residual repair policy trained on patient-disjoint splits.
- Learned verifier and selective iterative execution.
- Frozen-HRNet error-source evaluation.

## Current evidence

The program-aware oracle approximately halves both HVA and IMA error over four
synthetic noise levels. A geometry-only learned repair policy helps medium and
large corruptions but damages many lightly corrupted cases. Adding a verifier
reduces joint-protocol harm to roughly 2% on synthetic test corruptions and makes
multiple repair steps useful at higher noise.

The frozen HRNet landmark checkpoint is not an executable-geometry detector. On
the current patient-disjoint manifest, direct deterministic execution of its six
heatmap endpoints produces 52.61 degree HVA MAE and 71.96 degree IMA MAE. Selective
repair reduces these values, but not to a clinically useful level. Previous HRNet
results used a learned angle regressor after the landmarks, so they do not validate
the endpoints as a correct measurement construction.

This negative result defines the next method requirement: train the detector and
repair policy with executable measurement consistency, while preserving explicit
geometry and selective abstention.

### Learned-proposal decomposition

A five-fold patient-grouped experiment separates proposal quality from selection.
Across folds, unconditional learned repair worsens mean MAE from 2.801 to 2.890
degrees and harms 55.6% of cases. An oracle that selects among the same learned
component proposals improves mean MAE to 2.410 degrees with zero harm and succeeds
on 73.9% of cases. The proposals therefore contain useful corrections.

The current learned expected-gain selector does not transfer reliably across folds:
mean MAE is 2.842 degrees, mean harm is 8.6%, and coverage varies substantially
(15.7% mean, 23.7% standard deviation). Its mean protocol gain is negative. This
rules out treating a single uncalibrated gain-regression score as the stop rule.
The next selector must separate component ranking, positive-gain probability, and
conditional gain magnitude, followed by held-out probability calibration.

A subsequent five-fold risk-adjusted hurdle selector explicitly predicts component
ranking, benefit probability and magnitude, and harm probability and magnitude.
It controls overall harm at 7.7%, but still worsens MAE from 2.801 to 2.828 degrees.
Opportunity prevalence is stable at 81.0%, while opportunity recall is only 18.7%
and conditional harm among edited cases is 32.1%. Thus low overall harm is largely
the result of low coverage, not reliable intervention.

The likely experimental cause is proposal-selector leakage: selector targets are
currently computed from proposal outputs on the same cases used to fit the proposal.
The next experiment must use cross-fitted proposal predictions for selector training,
so every gain label is generated by a proposal model that did not train on that
patient. Calibration and test evaluation must remain on separate patient folds.

Nested patient-level cross-fitting disproved leakage as the primary explanation.
With every selector training label generated by an out-of-fold proposal, the method
reaches 2.850 degree MAE versus 2.801 without repair, with only 6.4% coverage and
6.4% opportunity recall. Meanwhile the oracle selector over the final learned
proposals reaches 2.296 degrees with stable 75.9% opportunity prevalence. The next
hypothesis is representational: a 64x64 global image vector is insufficient for
component diagnosis. The selector should receive axis-centered, orientation-aligned
local patches through a shared component encoder, alongside proposal displacement,
measurement sensitivity, and global protocol context.

Component-aligned patches alone did not solve selection (2.838 degree MAE). A more
useful signal comes from component disagreement across three independently trained
detectors. With ensemble-consensus axis proposals and a tabular gain model, the
conservative five-fold operating point improves every fold: mean MAE falls from
2.801 to 2.660 degrees, overall harm is 5.8%, and coverage is 17.5%. A less
conservative HGB point reaches 2.623 degrees at 8.1% harm and 22.3% coverage. The
oracle selector over identical consensus proposals reaches 2.035 degrees. These are
the first cross-validated end-to-end improvements, supporting epistemic component
uncertainty as a key selection signal while leaving substantial opportunity recall.

## Required ICLR experiments

1. Joint landmark, executable-angle, and topology-constrained detector training.
2. Image-conditioned repair proposals, compared with geometry-only repair.
3. Calibration and risk-coverage curves for accept/reject/stop decisions.
4. Real detector failures plus correlated, missing-point, rotation, and truncation
   corruptions; Gaussian noise alone is insufficient.
5. Cross-protocol transfer to Cobb angle without changing the executor architecture.
6. Baselines: direct regression, landmark-plus-analytic measurement, learned
   geometry regression, one-shot correction, ungated iterative repair, and oracle.
7. Three seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals, and both harm definitions:
   joint normalized protocol harm and any individual measurement harm.

## Decisive comparison

On identical real detector outputs and patient-disjoint splits, compare:

1. vanilla landmark refinement trained only with localization loss;
2. measurement-aware structured refinement with execution feedback, component
   verification, selective edits, and stopping;
3. scalar residual correction using the same image and initial geometry.

The central hypothesis is that minimizing landmark localization error is not the
same as repairing measurement-defining geometry. Report endpoint error, axis error,
measurement MAE, catastrophic error, calibration, harm, and risk-coverage.

For component `i`, expose the executor sensitivity
`S_i = ||dG(P,z)/dz_i||`. Repair priority should combine predicted component error
with measurement sensitivity, rather than simply selecting the landmark with the
largest localization error.

## Three gates

- **Recoverability:** a local oracle edit of one point or axis recovers a substantial
  fraction of real detector measurement error.
- **Learnability:** one-shot image-conditioned repair improves real detector errors;
  failure here rules out adding iterative complexity.
- **Comparative value:** structured repair must beat vanilla landmark refinement and
  offer more than a negligible scalar-MAE tie with scalar correction through safety,
  geometry accuracy, robustness, calibration, or cross-program transfer.

## Go / no-go criterion

Proceed with an ICLR submission only if executable-consistency training produces a
usable real-detector baseline and the verifier gives a meaningful risk-coverage
frontier on held-out patients. Synthetic-only gains are not sufficient.
